How it works

How do you find one door among 17,280 addresses?

Augustus Pell drew the street atlas that taxi drivers kept under the seat, and pencilled three unsolved cases into his last copy. Nobody in this city walks diagonally. Four steps, one pen, no knowledge of New York needed.

1

Read the statement

Every witness gives you one fact. None of them lies. Each fact rules out part of the city.

2

Strike out neighborhoods

Hatch the neighborhoods the clue eliminates, or count the squares on the grid.

3

Strike out streets

Six real streets per neighborhood. Cross the ones that no longer fit.

4

Cross out the numbers

Numbers 1 to 120 on every street. Keep going until a single door survives, then check the verification code.

The grid twist

This time, count the blocks.

Manhattan is drawn the New York way, avenues straight up the page, and cut into squares lettered A to F and numbered 1 to 22. Witnesses tell you how far they walked, up, down or across, never diagonally. Count the squares and strike out everything too near or too far. A worked example sits next to the grid of each case.

Every Murder Map book has one twist, taken from something real about its city. Same rules, same pen, a different way to close in.

Real streets

The real Manhattan, door by door.

24 genuine neighborhoods, from the Battery to Inwood. 144 real streets, six per neighborhood. 17,280 house numbers to cross out. Every clue is printed on the page: on the Hudson or the East River, downtown or above the park, a street named after a person, a number divisible by four.
