How it works

The board is the palace. The black king is the victim.

Every case is a real chess position, and every square belongs to a room of the palace. The white pieces are the guests, and all of them had a reason.

1

Set up the board

On a real chessboard, or scan the QR code on the case: the position opens on your phone, ready to play, with no engine.

2

Find every mate

Only one white piece moves, whatever Black tries. Several suspects can still pull it off. List them all.

3

Read the witnesses

The cook, the butler and the palace clockmaker saw who went where. From Chief Inspector on, one of them is lying.

4

Name the killer

Who gave mate, in which room, and how. Exactly one answer per case, checked by an independent solver before printing.

The rules in one minute

Find every mate. Then believe the witnesses.

The killer acts alone

Only one white piece moves. Black answers with anything he likes, and the killer still mates in the number of moves the case states. Several suspects can usually manage it on the board. That is the point.

Three sentences, no gossip

“X never left the Library.” Every square X touched was in the Library.
“Nobody set foot in the Stepwell.” No suspect passed through it.
“Somebody went through the Kitchens.” The killer crossed it at least once.

Who, where, how

Name the guest, the room where the mating move lands, and the kind of mate: back-rank, smothered, queen's kiss, or named after the piece. From Chief Inspector on, one witness is lying: find out who.

The series

One chess country per book. One chess rule per crime.

Same rules everywhere, a new palace, new suspects, and one chess rule that turns into a plot: promotion hides an impostor, a discovered check needs an accomplice, castling makes the perfect alibi. Each book stands alone.
