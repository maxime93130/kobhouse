How it works

How do you find one door among 16,560 addresses?

Detective Kanzaki found three case files in an unclaimed umbrella at the Lost and Found. Everything in this city has a number: the stations, the wards, the blocks. Four steps, one pen, no knowledge of Tokyo needed.

1

Read the statement

Every witness gives you one fact. None of them lies. Each fact rules out part of the city.

2

Strike out wards

Hatch the wards the clue eliminates, or ride the Yamanote loop and count the stations.

3

Strike out neighbourhoods

Six real neighbourhoods per ward, with their kanji. Cross the ones that no longer fit.

4

Cross out the blocks

Blocks 1 to 120 in every neighbourhood. Keep going until a single door survives, then check the verification code.

The Yamanote twist

This time, the train is a witness.

The Yamanote loop line circles the city with 30 stations. Witnesses tell you how far someone rode, clockwise or against the clock. Count the stations, cross out the wards they serve, and the loop closes in on the killer. A worked example sits next to the loop of each case.

Every Murder Map book has one twist, taken from something real about its city. Same rules, same pen, a different way to close in.

Why it works

The slow puzzle.

You'll know the rules in two minutes. You'll need about two hours per case. That is the point: one pen, one city, one thing on your mind.

Calm

A clear task, a simple gesture, progress you can see. Cross out the city one line at a time and let the rest of the day wait.

Focus

Finish

You don't guess the ending, you earn it

Fair
