Murder Map · How it works

Every witness tells the truth. None of them knows the address.

Read the statements in order. Each one is true, and each one lets you cross out part of the city. Four steps, one pen, no knowledge of the city needed.

1

Read the statement

Every witness gives you one fact. None of them lies. Each fact rules out part of the city.

2

Strike out districts

Hatch the districts the clue eliminates on the printed map. Trust the map, not your memory.

3

Strike out streets

Six real streets per district. Cross the ones that no longer fit.

4

Cross out the numbers

Numbers 1 to 120 on every street. Keep going until a single door survives, then check the verification code.

Murder Map · Why it works

The slow puzzle.

You'll know the rules in two minutes. You'll need about two hours per case. That is the point: one pen, one city, one thing on your mind.

Calm

Calm is a method

A clear task, a simple gesture, progress you can see. Cross out the city one line at a time and let the rest of the day wait.

Focus

Two hours where your phone doesn't exist

Paper, pen, sixteen statements. Nothing to swipe, nothing to check, nothing to charge.

Finish

You don't guess the ending, you earn it

Every hatched district and every crossed-out number is work you did. When one door is left, the code at the back confirms it.

Fair

Hard, never unfair

Murder Map · One twist per city

Same rules. A different way to close in.

Every city gets one mechanic of its own, taken from something real: a loop line, a street grid. The books stand alone and can be played in any order.

Paris

The original

Rive gauche or rive droite, along the Seine or by the périphérique: twenty arrondissements and the clues to cut them down, one statement at a time.

Tokyo

The train is a witness

The Yamanote loop circles the city with 30 stations. Witnesses tell you how far someone rode, clockwise or against the clock, and the loop closes in on the killer.

New York

Count the blocks

Manhattan on a lettered grid, avenues straight up the page. Witnesses tell you how far they walked, up, down or across, never diagonally.
